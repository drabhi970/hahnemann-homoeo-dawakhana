Thyroid Health: Common Symptoms, Useful Tests, and When to Seek Medical Care

Thyroid problems are common and may affect energy, mood, weight, sleep, digestion, heart rate, and menstrual health. Understanding the symptoms and getting appropriate medical evaluation can help people take timely action.

What does the thyroid gland do?

The thyroid is a small gland in the neck that produces hormones involved in metabolism and many body functions. When the gland produces too little hormone, the condition is commonly called hypothyroidism. When it produces too much hormone, it may be called hyperthyroidism.

Symptoms can overlap with stress, poor sleep, nutritional deficiencies, medication effects, and other health conditions. This is why symptoms alone cannot confirm a thyroid disorder.

Common symptoms of an underactive thyroid

Some people with hypothyroidism may notice:

  • Persistent tiredness or low energy
  • Feeling unusually cold
  • Dry skin or increased hair fall
  • Constipation
  • Difficulty concentrating or feeling mentally slowed down
  • Weight gain or difficulty losing weight
  • Low mood or reduced motivation
  • Heavy or irregular menstrual periods
  • Muscle aches, cramps, or joint discomfort

Common symptoms of an overactive thyroid

Hyperthyroidism may be associated with:

  • Fast or irregular heartbeat
  • Feeling restless, anxious, or unusually irritable
  • Increased sweating or heat intolerance
  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Hand tremors
  • Frequent bowel movements
  • Difficulty sleeping
  • Muscle weakness
  • Changes in menstrual cycles

Which tests are commonly used?

A clinician may recommend blood tests based on symptoms, examination findings, age, pregnancy status, and medical history. Commonly used tests may include:

  • TSH: often used as an initial thyroid function test
  • Free T4: helps assess the amount of available thyroid hormone
  • Free T3: may be useful in selected cases, especially when overactive thyroid is suspected
  • Thyroid antibody tests: may help assess autoimmune thyroid conditions
  • Ultrasound: may be advised when there is a neck swelling, nodule, or other structural concern

Test selection and interpretation should be done by a qualified healthcare professional. Results can be affected by pregnancy, recent illness, medicines, and certain supplements.

Everyday habits that support general thyroid health

Healthy routines do not replace diagnosis or treatment, but they can support overall wellbeing:

  • Eat regular, balanced meals with adequate protein and a variety of fruits and vegetables.
  • Take prescribed medicines exactly as directed and do not change the dose on your own.
  • Keep follow-up appointments and complete repeat tests when advised.
  • Prioritise regular sleep and gentle physical activity as tolerated.
  • Tell your clinician about supplements, herbal products, and over-the-counter medicines.
  • Avoid taking high-dose iodine supplements unless specifically advised by a clinician.

When should you seek urgent medical care?

Seek urgent medical help if thyroid-related symptoms occur with:

  • Chest pain, severe breathlessness, or fainting
  • A very fast or irregular heartbeat with weakness or dizziness
  • Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or a sudden change in mental status
  • Severe swelling in the neck with difficulty breathing or swallowing
  • Sudden worsening in a person who is pregnant or has a known thyroid condition
