Thyroid Health: Symptoms, Tests & Healthy Lifestyle Tips

The thyroid is a small, butterfly-shaped gland located at the front of the neck. Thyroid hormones influence how the body uses energy and affect many body functions.

Thyroid disorders can occur when the thyroid produces too little hormone, known as hypothyroidism, or too much hormone, known as hyperthyroidism.

Important: Thyroid symptoms can overlap with many other health conditions. Symptoms alone cannot confirm a thyroid disorder. Appropriate medical evaluation and thyroid testing may be necessary.

Symptoms of an Underactive Thyroid

Hypothyroidism can develop gradually, and symptoms vary from person to person.

  • Tiredness or low energy
  • Unexplained weight gain
  • Feeling unusually cold
  • Dry skin
  • Dry or thinning hair
  • Constipation
  • Muscle or joint discomfort
  • Changes in menstrual periods
  • Low mood
  • Slower heart rate

Symptoms of an Overactive Thyroid

Hyperthyroidism may produce symptoms such as:

  • Unexplained weight loss
  • Feeling unusually hot
  • Increased sweating
  • Fast or irregular heartbeat
  • Nervousness or restlessness
  • Difficulty sleeping
  • Trembling
  • Increased bowel movements

Which Tests Check Thyroid Function?

Blood tests are commonly used to evaluate thyroid function. Depending on the individual situation, a healthcare professional may recommend:

  • TSH – thyroid-stimulating hormone
  • Free T4 – free thyroxine
  • T3 – particularly useful in some cases of suspected hyperthyroidism
  • Thyroid antibody tests – when an autoimmune thyroid condition is suspected

TSH is commonly used as an initial thyroid function test, with additional testing performed when appropriate.

What About Thyroid Nodules?

A thyroid nodule is a lump within the thyroid gland. When a nodule is suspected, a healthcare professional may recommend further assessment.

A thyroid ultrasound can help evaluate a thyroid nodule and determine whether additional investigation may be necessary.

Healthy Lifestyle Habits

A healthy lifestyle can support general wellbeing while a thyroid condition is being evaluated or managed.

  • Eat a balanced and varied diet.
  • Maintain regular physical activity according to your health and ability.
  • Get adequate sleep.
  • Attend regular medical appointments when you have a diagnosed thyroid condition.
  • Take prescribed thyroid medication exactly as directed.
  • Discuss supplements, particularly iodine supplements, with your healthcare professional before taking them.
Important about iodine:

Do not start iodine supplements or high-dose iodine products without appropriate medical advice. Excessive iodine can affect thyroid function in some people.

When Should You See a Doctor?

Consider medical evaluation if you have persistent symptoms suggestive of thyroid disease, unexplained changes in weight, persistent changes in heart rate, menstrual changes, swelling in the neck, or other symptoms that concern you.

A diagnosis should not be made from symptoms alone. Blood tests and, when necessary, other investigations can help determine whether a thyroid disorder is present and identify its possible cause.

Thyroid Treatment

Treatment depends on the specific thyroid disorder and its underlying cause.

For hypothyroidism, standard medical treatment commonly involves thyroid hormone replacement such as levothyroxine. If you are taking prescribed thyroid medication, do not stop or change the dose without discussing it with the healthcare professional managing your treatment.
