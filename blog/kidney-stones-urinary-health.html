Kidney Stones: Symptoms, Prevention & When to Seek Urgent Care

Kidney stones can cause severe pain when a stone moves through the urinary tract. Some stones pass naturally, while others require medical treatment. The right approach depends on the stone's size, location and the person's condition.

Common symptoms

  • Sharp pain in the side, back or lower abdomen
  • Pain that may come and go in waves
  • Burning or discomfort while urinating
  • Blood in the urine
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Frequent or urgent urination

Urgent warning signs

  • Fever or chills with urinary symptoms
  • Severe or worsening pain
  • Repeated vomiting or inability to keep fluids down
  • Very little or no urine
  • Feeling faint or severely unwell

Prevention basics

  • Adequate fluid intake is important for many people, unless fluid restriction has been advised.
  • Stone prevention depends on the type of stone and may require specific dietary advice.
  • People with recurrent stones should discuss evaluation and prevention with a healthcare professional.

When should you seek professional advice?

Consider a medical consultation when symptoms are persistent, recurrent, worsening, affecting daily life, or accompanied by unexplained changes in weight, appetite, sleep, bleeding, urination, breathing or general wellbeing. A qualified healthcare professional can decide whether examination, testing or referral is appropriate.
