Respiratory Health: Cough, Breathing Problems & Warning Signs

Respiratory symptoms such as cough, wheezing, nasal congestion and shortness of breath are common. They may occur with infections, allergies, asthma, environmental irritation and several other conditions.

Important: The cause of a respiratory symptom cannot always be determined from the symptom alone. Persistent, recurrent or severe symptoms should be evaluated by a qualified healthcare professional.

Common Respiratory Symptoms

Respiratory problems can present in different ways. Common symptoms include:

  • Cough, either dry or with mucus
  • Blocked or runny nose
  • Sneezing
  • Wheezing or noisy breathing
  • Chest tightness
  • Shortness of breath
  • Sore throat
  • Repeated respiratory infections

What Can Cause a Cough?

Cough can have many causes. Viral respiratory infections are common, but allergies, asthma, smoking or exposure to smoke, air pollution, acid reflux and some medicines can also contribute.

A cough that lasts longer than expected, keeps returning or is associated with other concerning symptoms should be medically evaluated.

Asthma and Breathing Symptoms

Asthma can cause episodes of wheezing, coughing, chest tightness and difficulty breathing. Symptoms may be triggered by allergens, respiratory infections, exercise, cold air, smoke or other factors.

If asthma is suspected, proper assessment is important. People who already have an asthma diagnosis should follow their prescribed treatment plan and keep any recommended reliever medication available.

Healthy Habits for Respiratory Health

  • Avoid smoking and second-hand smoke.
  • Reduce exposure to dust, fumes and other known irritants where possible.
  • Keep indoor spaces adequately ventilated.
  • Wash hands regularly, especially during periods of respiratory infections.
  • Maintain regular physical activity according to your health status.
  • Get adequate sleep and maintain a balanced diet.
  • If you have known allergies or asthma, try to identify and reduce exposure to your individual triggers.

When Should a Cough Be Checked?

Seek professional medical advice if a cough is persistent, repeatedly returns, interferes with sleep or daily activities, or is accompanied by symptoms such as fever, wheezing, chest discomfort or unexplained weight loss.

Seek urgent medical care if you have severe difficulty breathing, blue or grey lips or skin, severe chest pain, confusion, fainting, coughing up significant amounts of blood, or rapidly worsening breathing symptoms.

Children and Respiratory Symptoms

Children can develop coughs and breathing problems from infections, allergies, asthma and other causes. Parents or caregivers should pay attention to changes in breathing, activity level, hydration and general behaviour.

Rapid or difficult breathing, severe lethargy, bluish skin or lips, inability to drink, or significant worsening of symptoms requires prompt medical attention.

Air Pollution and Respiratory Health

Air pollution can irritate the respiratory system and may worsen symptoms in people with asthma or other respiratory conditions.

During periods of poor air quality, consider reducing prolonged outdoor exposure and following local public-health recommendations, particularly if you have an existing respiratory condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every cough caused by an infection?

No. Cough can have many causes, including infections, allergies, asthma, reflux, environmental irritation and certain medicines.

Can allergies cause breathing symptoms?

Allergies can cause symptoms such as sneezing, nasal congestion, runny nose and, in some people, asthma-related breathing symptoms.

When is breathing difficulty an emergency?

Severe or rapidly worsening breathing difficulty, blue or grey lips or skin, severe chest pain, confusion or fainting should be treated as an emergency and requires immediate medical attention.
