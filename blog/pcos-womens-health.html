PCOS & Women's Health: Symptoms, Lifestyle & When to Seek Help

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal and metabolic condition that can affect menstrual cycles, ovulation, skin and hair, and metabolic health. Symptoms can vary considerably from one person to another.

Important: PCOS is a medical condition that should be properly evaluated. Similar symptoms can occur with other health conditions, so a diagnosis should not be based on symptoms alone.

What is PCOS?

PCOS is a condition associated with hormonal changes and problems with ovulation. Some people with PCOS may have irregular periods, acne, increased facial or body hair, scalp hair thinning or difficulty becoming pregnant.

Having ovarian cysts is not required for a person to have PCOS, and the condition can present differently in different individuals.

Common Symptoms of PCOS

Symptoms may include:

  • Irregular, infrequent or absent menstrual periods
  • Acne or oily skin
  • Increased facial or body hair
  • Thinning hair on the scalp
  • Changes in body weight
  • Difficulty with ovulation or conception
  • Darkened or thickened skin in some areas

Not everyone with PCOS experiences all of these symptoms.

Why Evaluation Matters

Irregular periods or other symptoms can have several possible causes. A healthcare professional may consider menstrual history, symptoms, physical findings and appropriate laboratory or imaging tests when evaluating a person.

Early evaluation can also help identify associated metabolic concerns, such as problems with blood sugar, cholesterol or blood pressure, when these are relevant.

Healthy Lifestyle Habits

Lifestyle measures can be an important part of managing overall health in people with PCOS.
