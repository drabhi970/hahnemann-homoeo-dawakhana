Dyslexia and Learning Difficulties in Children: Signs, Causes and Support

Dyslexia is a common learning difficulty that primarily affects reading, spelling and the ability to connect speech sounds with letters and words. It can occur in children with normal intelligence and vision and may sometimes remain unrecognized until later childhood or adulthood.

What causes dyslexia?

Dyslexia is associated with differences in the way the brain processes language and written information. Genetic factors also play an important role, and children with a family history of dyslexia may have a higher likelihood of developing it.

Early signs of dyslexia

Before starting school, some children may show:

  • Delayed speech or language development
  • Difficulty learning new words
  • Difficulty learning nursery rhymes
  • Difficulty recognizing or producing rhyming words
  • Difficulty remembering sequences

Signs during school years

As reading instruction begins, children may experience:

  • Reading below the expected level for their age
  • Difficulty connecting letters with sounds
  • Difficulty sounding out unfamiliar words
  • Difficulty understanding or remembering spoken instructions
  • Difficulty remembering sequences
  • Problems with spelling and reading fluency

Dyslexia in teenagers and adults

Older children and adults may continue to experience difficulties with reading aloud, spelling, summarizing information, learning new languages, memorization and time management.

What should parents do?

Early identification can make a significant difference. If you notice persistent reading or learning difficulties, consider discussing them with your child's pediatrician, psychologist, developmental specialist or qualified educational professional. A proper assessment can identify the child's specific learning needs.

Support may include structured reading instruction, individualized educational strategies, classroom accommodations and other interventions recommended after assessment.

Role of Homoeopathy

Homoeopathic consultation may be considered by families as a complementary approach to overall wellbeing, but it should not replace educational intervention, developmental assessment, or specialist care for dyslexia. There is currently insufficient reliable evidence that homoeopathic medicines can treat or cure dyslexia itself.
